## 1. Methodology

This evaluation applies a researcher-administered rubric to all recommendations generated by the post-fix `/api/assess` pipeline. Following the correction of the recommendation wiring defect (commit `6e425dd`), the LLM's `recommendations` field is now correctly passed to `calculateScores()` and returned in the API response.

The evaluation examines whether the post-fix recommendations are:
1. Grounded in source document evidence
2. Consistent with the scored assessment weaknesses
3. Specific to the company's situation
4. Actionable for an SME

### Evaluation scope

This is a **controlled robustness evaluation** — not a multi-company study. All three fixtures represent the same underlying company (Meridian Manufacturing Ltd) with identical financial facts, tested under three different document presentation conditions. Results characterise whether recommendation quality remains grounded and consistent when the same information is presented as clean structured prose, tabular two-column format, and OCR-degraded text.

**These results cannot be generalised to other companies, sectors, or document types.**

## 5. Rubric Definition

Each recommendation is scored on four criteria, each scored **0, 1, or 2**. Maximum score per recommendation: **8 points**.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Evidence-Grounded (EG)** | No identifiable support in source document or assessment evidence | Partially or indirectly supported | Clearly supported by specific source information or assessment evidence |
| **Score-Consistent (SC)** | Contradicts the assessment or targets an area without evidence of weakness | Broadly compatible with the assessment but weakly connected to an identified weakness | Clearly addresses a specific weakness identified by metrics, digital maturity, or calculated scores |
| **Specific (SP)** | Generic advice applicable to almost any SME | Identifies a business area but remains broad | Gives a specific improvement direction connected to the company's situation |
| **Actionable (AC)** | Too vague to guide action | Suggests a broad action but provides limited implementation direction | Gives a practical action an SME could reasonably consider |

**Application notes:**
- Scores are not awarded for professional tone, fluent language, or plausibility alone.
- Evidence must be traceable to the API response or source document.
- Ambiguities are explained in the justification column.
- Known underlying weaknesses (operating margin 0%, revenue concentration 68%, COGS +9.1%) are referenced from Fixture A (clean baseline) as ground truth; identical facts apply to B and C.

### Recommendation content variation

Post-fix recommendations are **not identical across fixtures**. Each fixture produced a distinct set:

| Fixture | Recommendation focus |
|---|---|
| A — Clean | Financial: raw material cost control, pricing strategy, customer diversification |
| B — Tabular | Digital tool utilisation: SYSPRO MES production, SAP supply chain, Salesforce CRM |

This represents a fundamental change from the pre-fix behaviour where all three fixtures returned identical hardcoded fallback strings.

### Quality trend across conditions

| Metric | A (Clean) | B (Tabular) | C (OCR-Degraded) | Trend |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| Overall % | 75.0% | 70.8% | **83.3%** | C > A > B |

**Key finding — Fixture B:** The tabular format caused the lowest evidence-grounded and score-consistency scores (1.00/2.00 each). The LLM shifted to tool-centric recommendations when the tabular financial data was harder to parse. These recommendations are highly specific (SP=2.00) and actionable (AC=1.67) but less well-evidenced and less directly connected to the primary financial weaknesses.

**Key finding — Fixture C:** Despite heavy OCR corruption, Fixture C achieved the highest overall score (83.3%). The LLM successfully extracted specific figures (9.1%, 68%) from corrupted text and cited them explicitly, producing recommendations that score EG=2 in all three cases. This is a counter-intuitive result: OCR degradation did not degrade recommendation quality — it may have made the financial narrative sections more prominent relative to the corrupted structured data.

## 10. Contradictions Between Recommendations and Assessment Evidence

### Digital maturity classification — persistent contradiction

All three fixtures classify `digitalMaturityLevel = "Medium"` despite:
- Five enterprise-grade tools extracted in every fixture
- Source document explicitly stating digital maturity is **HIGH**

**Affected:** This inconsistency does not appear to have influenced the post-fix recommendations. None of the nine recommendations mention digital maturity level. However, it represents a persistent classification error unrelated to the wiring fix.

### Fixture B — tool recommendations vs. operating margin

Fixture B's three recommendations address SYSPRO MES, SAP S/4HANA, and Salesforce CRM, but make no reference to the operating margin weakness (0% vs 12%). The grossMargin extraction error (−100) in Fixture B may have caused the LLM to lose confidence in the financial figures and default to the more clearly readable digital tools section. This creates a gap between the most critical identified weakness and the generated recommendations.

This is an **honest limitation**: the tabular format caused a qualitative shift in recommendation focus away from the primary financial weakness.

### Fixture B — "improve sales and customer service" (B3)

Recommending CRM enhancement to "improve sales and customer service" does not clearly connect to any identified metric weakness. Revenue per employee (£100,000) and output per payroll (4.0x) are both above sector median, implying no obvious sales underperformance. The recommendation is plausible as a strategic action but is not grounded in an identified weakness. SC=1 reflects this ambiguity.

## 11. Limitations

1. **Single company, controlled conditions.** All three fixtures represent Meridian Manufacturing Ltd. This evaluation characterises robustness across document conditions, not quality across different business profiles. Results cannot be generalised to other sectors, company sizes, or financial situations.

2. **Single evaluation run per fixture.** The LLM uses `temperature: 0.0` and `seed: 42`, producing deterministic output for identical inputs. Each fixture was run once; additional runs would produce identical output under these settings.

3. **Researcher-administered rubric only.** All rubric scores were applied by the researcher conducting this evaluation. No independent co-rater reviewed the scores. This limits inter-rater reliability claims.

4. **Recommendations not conditioned on computed scores.** The LLM generates recommendations in the same call as metric extraction, using only the raw document text. It does not receive `labourEfficiencyScore`, `financialHealthScore`, or `productivityIndex` as input. Post-fix recommendations are document-responsive but not directly conditioned on the computed quantitative outputs.

5. **Tabular grossMargin error unresolved.** The grossMargin extraction error (−100) for Fixture B remains unfixed — this is a separate extraction defect outside the scope of the recommendation wiring fix. Its effect on recommendation quality is observable in this evaluation (Fixture B scores lowest overall).

6. **Three fixtures represent a small evaluation set.** Nine total recommendations across three controlled conditions provides directional evidence but not statistically robust conclusions.
